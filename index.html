About me

I am Adjunct Professor and Postdoctoral Researcher at DIBRIS – Department of Informatics, Bioengineering, Robotics & Systems Engineering, University of Genoa, Italy. My research focuses on the intersection of machine learning, deep learning, computer vision, artificial intelligence and circular economy with a specialized focus on industrial automation and sustainable technology.

I hold a PhD in Computer Science from the University of Genoa (2025), where I developed the state-of-the-art V-PCB Dataset (patented) and deep learning-powered computer vision system for the selective disassembly of waste printed circuit boards.

I have been an invited speaker at international venues including the Global Summit on AI (2025). As an international collaborator, I visited the Institute of Computer Science, Faculty of Mathematics, Physics, and Computer Science, University of Opole, Poland in 2025 and gave an invited talk on "AI-powered Innovations for Circular Economy". In August 2026, I organized a Special Session on "AI-Powered Innovations for Circular Economy" at IEEE RTSI 2026 at Aalto University, Espoo, Finland.

Currently, I contribute to the EU-funded VALOR project (Interreg Marittimo IT-FR), where I lead the development of AI-powered vision systems for the selective disassembly and automated sorting of electronic waste. My work is deeply rooted in advancing the European Green Deal and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). I am an active member of the IEEE Computer Society. I am also hosting a Special Topic in Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence journal titled "AI for Circular Economy: Intelligent Algorithms for Sustainable Resource Recovery"

Extraction of Critical Raw Materials From Waste Printed Circuit Boards Using Machine Learning and Computer Vision
Muhammad Mohsin, Stefano Rovetta, Francesco Masulli, Alberto Cabri
IEEE Access, Vol. 14, pp. 50230–50245, 2026
DOI Abstract BibTeX
Abstract
The extraction of critical raw materials (CRMs) from waste printed circuit boards (WPCBs) represents a crucial challenge for the economy, particularly in the electronics sector. To improve the concentration of individual CRMs and facilitate their extraction from WPCBs, we propose an innovative approach based on machine learning and computer vision to guide mechatronics system in selectively disassembling various types of electronic components from WPCBs. Our approach addresses the need for a robust and automated solution to identify and localize valuable components that contain CRMs. Specifically, we use YOLOv5 and YOLOv8 models, which are the state of the art convolutional neural networks (CNN) for detection and localization tasks. Our pipeline, called EC-Det, starts from collecting a custom image set called V-PCB, and obtains a large collection of 10,829 high-quality images of WPCBs. These images are used to train the YOLO family variants which show excellent accuracy. Furthermore, we consider not only accuracy but also memory usage, training time, and inference time for our CNN models, evaluating their suitability for resource-constrained on the edge systems. Our results show that integration of machine vision system provides a highly effective and reproducible solution for guiding automated mechatronics system, thereby enhancing the efficiency of the CRMs extraction process.

A Systematic Review of Deep Learning Approaches for Hepatopancreatic Tumor Segmentation
R Hussain, Muhammad Mohsin, D Khan, M Zohaib
Journal of Imaging, 2026
PDF Abstract BibTeX
Abstract
Hepatopancreatic tumors present a critical imaging diagnostic challenge due to varying structures and sizes. This systematic review synthesizes recent developments in artificial intelligence and deep learning methods specifically targeted toward automated segmentation of hepatopancreatic tumors. We review state-of-the-art CNNs and visual transformers (ViTs), contrast dataset benchmarks, and discuss research challenges including pixel imbalances and clinical workflow deployment obstacles.

"AI for Circular Economy: Intelligent Algorithms for Sustainable Resource Recovery"

Research Topic · Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence (AI in Business section)

This Research Topic addresses urgent sustainability challenges by combining artificial intelligence with waste management. It focuses on AI algorithms and sustainable innovation to advance circular economy practices, particularly for electronic waste, battery recycling, and critical raw material recovery. Submissions should demonstrate how computational methods tackle real-world resource management challenges aligned with international sustainability goals.
